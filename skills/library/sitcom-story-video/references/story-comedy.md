# Story Design and Sitcom Comedy

Use this reference when developing the premise, scene beats, dialogue, or edit timing.

## Comic engine

Choose one durable engine that can produce several beats without changing the world: status mismatch, literal-minded helper, secret everyone can see, overconfident plan, incompatible rules, or escalating prop malfunction. Give each character a conflicting want so jokes come from behavior, not random one-liners.

## Short-form structure

For a 45-90 second short, use a compact arc:

1. **Cold open (0-5 s)**: show an odd rule, visual problem, or impossible claim immediately.
2. **Setup (5-20 s)**: establish who wants what and the simple plan.
3. **Escalation (20-50 s)**: repeat the plan with a larger consequence; vary the shot scale or blocking each time.
4. **Reversal (50-70 s)**: reveal the misunderstood rule or let the least competent character be accidentally right.
5. **Tag (last 3-8 s)**: a final reaction or visual callback that reframes the opening.

For longer pieces, repeat the escalation cycle once with a new tactic, not a new premise.

## Beat writing

Every beat should specify:

- objective: what the speaker is trying to get;
- obstacle: what blocks it;
- behavior: the visible, filmable choice;
- turn: what changes the audience's expectation;
- reaction: whose face/body sells the joke.

Write dialogue for breath and performance, usually 5-14 words per line. Put exposition in a prop, glance, or action when possible. Use a pause before the reveal and a clean reaction after it. Avoid explaining the joke in captions unless the format requires subtitles.

## Scene and shot design

Keep one primary location and a small cast for visual continuity. Introduce a prop early, give it a rule, then make the characters misuse that rule. A shot should do one of three jobs: establish, complicate, or pay off. Reaction shots are not filler: they are the audience's laugh track and should be explicitly scripted.

Use visual callbacks: repeat the same composition or prop with one changed detail at the tag. Escalate by consequence, scale, social embarrassment, or loss of control; do not escalate only by louder dialogue.

## Dialogue and audio checks

Bind every line to a visible speaker and keep one speaker active per shot where lip sync matters. Use off-screen lines only when the speaker's location is unambiguous. Mark mouth-state needs (speaking, closed-mouth reaction, laugh, gasp) in animation prompts if the model supports it. Keep room tone continuous across cuts and duck music under dialogue.

## Failure diagnosis

- **Not funny**: sharpen the conflicting wants or add a specific rule to the prop.
- **Confusing**: restate geography through a master shot and simplify the action.
- **Flat pacing**: shorten setup, add a reaction hold, or move the reversal earlier.
- **AI uncanny**: reduce simultaneous actions, use a closer reference image, and lock hands/props in the continuity ledger.
- **Ending weak**: write the tag first, then plant its visual callback in the cold open.
