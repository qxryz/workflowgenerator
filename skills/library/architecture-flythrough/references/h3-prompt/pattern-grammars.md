# Architectural Pattern Grammars

Use only a pattern that supports the approved route. These grammars describe motion within the existing take, not new camera instructions.

## Landmark approach

Establish location and recognizable silhouette → approach with readable scale → reveal distinguishing components → settle on the approved composition. Preserve exact relative positions of other landmarks.

## Urban corridor

Establish the skyline → descend into the approved street or building corridor → use foreground parallax and controlled speed changes → emerge into the planned view. Do not move buildings to clear the camera or add unrelated obstacles.

## Exterior to interior

Maintain one connected spatial route → approach a verified opening → transition through its real clearance → reveal the approved interior. Follow the Bible's allowed exposure and local-light transition while retaining the world-space sun. Mark inferred interiors in production records.

## Close architectural pass

Use the approved working distance and lens to reveal facade scale, material, and structural rhythm. Protect near-camera geometry, straight edges, stable openings, and foreground/background occlusion. Do not substitute random shake for speed.

## Multiple landmarks

Give each building a stable name, position, silhouette, and characteristic components. Prevent swapping, merging, duplication, deletion, or generic replacement. Add explicit text or signage only when required by the user; do not turn the final composition into a branded product presentation.
