Tier 3 is liquid, ice, fruit, and botanical motion: use motivated fluid waves, curved ballistic paths, varied rotations, delayed reactions, and readable depth separation.

Tier 4 is dimensional typography and set architecture: heavy, rigid, restrained, using slow parallax and controlled edge-light reveals.

Tier 5 is micro-detail: condensation, bubbles, fine mist, small droplets, frost particles, and subtle reflections.

CAUSE AND EFFECT

Fine droplets and bubbles continue as tertiary motion. Typography is revealed through light and parallax. Everything resolves into a stable final product lockup.

Do not start every movement at the same time. Large objects accelerate and decelerate more slowly than small objects.

MATERIAL RULES

Metal packaging remains rigid with controlled reflections and stable label textures. Condensation adheres to the surface with varied droplet sizes and occasional slow downward movement. Liquid uses coherent sheets, surface tension, gravity, inertia, accurate refraction, and natural droplet separation. Ice has hard crystalline geometry, weight, internal refraction, and controlled rotation. Fruit has organic weight, moist highlights, and curved ballistic motion. Botanical stems use delayed flex and subtle spring response. Dimensional typography remains heavy, stable, and correctly spelled.

5.5-9.0s: Energy build. Introduce the main liquid action, delayed fruit, ice, botanical, bubble, or droplet response, stronger parallax, and a controlled speed ramp.

9.0-12.0s: Visual climax. Deliver the largest but still readable fluid, ingredient, and lighting moment. Use a brief high-speed-photography near-freeze while keeping the label visible.

12.0-15.0s: Hero resolution. Reduce secondary motion, let elements settle, move into the strongest final angle, and hold the product sharply for at least the final 1.5 to 2.0 seconds.
