## Extracted principles for this Skill

### Micro-expression timing

- Micro expressions are very brief facial expressions, often occurring in half a second or less.
- They are useful as a prompt concept because they suggest emotional leakage, concealment, and involuntary flashes.
- Macro expressions last longer and usually match what is being said; micro expressions often reveal what the character tries to hide.

Prompt implication: do not write only a static expression. Write a quick leakage beat, a concealment beat, and a recovery beat.

### Emotion families

- Public Ekman materials commonly discuss seven universal emotion categories: anger, contempt, disgust, enjoyment, fear, sadness, and surprise.
- Emotions are not single fixed states; they exist as families and variations.
- Triggers may be actual, imagined, remembered, or socially induced.

Prompt implication: route broad phrases into emotion families, then add contradiction and context, such as sadness + dignity, anger + politeness, fear + social mask, enjoyment + disbelief.

### Facial Action Coding System influence

- FACS describes visible facial movement through Action Units and combinations.
- The Skill should not claim FACS certification or use exact AU scoring as diagnosis.
- It can borrow the idea of decomposing expression into visible regions: eyes, brows, mouth, jaw, cheeks, nose, throat/breath, head/neck, and timing.

Prompt implication: replace vague labels like “sad face” with observable components: eyelid weight, inner brow pull, mouth-corner resistance, jaw lock, swallow, breath leak, delayed blink.

### Display rules and concealment

- Facial expressions may be universal and culture-specific; display rules affect when, how, and to whom emotions are shown.
- Concealment creates the most useful acting prompt structure: mask versus leak.

Prompt implication: every nuanced performance should include “what the character tries to show” and “what leaks through”.

### Gesture types

- Illustrator gestures accompany speech and show emphasis or thought flow.
- Manipulator gestures involve self-touch, grooming, rubbing, pinching, holding, scratching, or interacting with props; they often rise with discomfort but should not be treated as proof of deception.
- Emblems are deliberate culturally meaningful gestures; emblematic slips can reveal hidden information.

Prompt implication: use hand/prop actions as acting leakage only when plausible: fingertip pressure, smoothing fabric, rubbing thumb and finger, gripping a cup, stopping a reach, over-neat arrangement of an object.

## Usage rule

When generating a prompt, combine:

1. one source principle from this note;
2. 1–3 acting prototypes from `performance-prototype-library.md`;
3. 1–3 emotion routes from `emotion-route-library.md`;
4. 3–8 visible chains from `muscle-dispatch-library.md`.

Then rewrite them into a compact, source-preserving acting prompt in the user's language. Do not cite sources in the user's final creative prompt unless the user asks for the research basis.
